Re: Application for the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program, Electrical Engineering

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am applying for the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program in electrical engineering. I complete a Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at UTS in November 2026. I recently finished twelve weeks as the electrical and measurement engineer on a medical instrumentation project, delivered through Optik Consultancy for the Respiratory Investigation Unit at Royal North Shore Hospital.

Industrus fits the electrical engineering work I want to do. Your advisory work on Australia’s largest lithium-ion battery is the kind of project I have studied from the network side: in a power systems studio project, my team modelled a 50 MW battery on a transmission test system and showed that it cleared the only voltage violation under the worst contingency. A program that rotates through consulting projects would let me apply that in practice. My response to each criterion follows.

Selection criterion 1A commitment to ethical conduct and the highest standards of professional accountability

At the project showcase I told the client that the patient measurements from my device were wrong, and explained why, instead of presenting the passing bench result alone. Two weeks before handover, the device was passing its daily system check while producing human measurements that varied by over 50 percent between breaths. I owned the calibration, so whether the instrument could be trusted was my call.

I found that both check points sat on the end nodes of my own calibration curve, so the check was validating the correction against its own data and could not fail. I then proved the correction invalid under breathing flow three separate ways before reporting it. The client’s reference letter records that this disclosure is the main reason he trusted the figures we did give him.

Selection criterion 2Demonstrated ability to effectively communicate both with other engineers and with stakeholders from different fields

I turned the hospital’s clinical needs into an electrical specification the team could design and test against. In the first weeks our discussions kept moving to circuit design before anyone had confirmed what the hospital needed, or why its existing device fell short.

I held the design work back and put specific questions to the respiratory physicians in their own terms: what they measured now, what the existing device could not do, and what accuracy would be clinically useful. Inside the team I translated the other way, building the calibration and system check screens with the software developers. The specification settled on a single frequency measurement at 6 Hz, matched to the client group’s own reference device, so that our bench results could be checked against reference data they supplied. The client agreed to it.

Selection criterion 3The ability to engage with a creative, innovative and proactive environment

I designed a start-of-day check that lets clinical staff confirm the device is in a known condition before any patient session, although it sat outside the brief. Two earlier student groups had attempted this problem without returning a working measurement, and the brief specified a device but said nothing about how staff would know each morning whether it could be used.

I studied how commercial oscillometry systems handle this, then designed a check that measures a known load and reports pass or fail against tolerance before any recording can begin. I built it into the operating software with the software team. At the showcase the device passed its own check with both measurements within 8 percent of expected, and the session moved straight to live measurement.

Selection criterion 4Demonstrated ability to use and manage information

I turned three months of working records into a handover package that a team I will never meet can continue from. At handover the client asked for an operating procedure, enough technical detail for a different team to continue, and the raw data.

I had kept dated technical records at each significant step, around twenty documents between May and August, so the evidence was already assembled. I wrote the handover report, compiled the final design report from the sub-teams’ contributions, kept the analysis scripts with the recordings they were run on, and packaged the software to run on a hospital laptop without a Python installation. The report opens with the current condition of the device, including the calibration limitation and a fault that stops the 5 Hz channel working. The client wrote that placing those limits first is what makes it useful to his group.

Selection criterion 5The ability to manage your own performance in a professional environment

With two weeks of the placement left, I stopped trying to repair the calibration correction and spent the time proving why it could not be fixed in the time available. A correct fix needed a closed circuit reference rig calibrated in the patient’s own flow regime, which did not exist and could not be built in two weeks.

I made that decision on the evidence, not because time felt short. The project reached the showcase with a working instrument and a documented account of exactly where the measurement is and is not valid. I had assumed the hard part would be the hardware. The more useful judgement was knowing when to stop building.

Selection criterion 6A demonstrated ability to work as part of a team and to show leadership when required

When the team lost track of how the measurement work fed the other streams, I took on coordinating the technical dependencies between them. I was the only electrical engineer on a six person team that also covered biomedical, software, data and mechanical engineering, and the measurement work the rest of the design depended on was mine.

I split the work to match each member’s discipline and reviewed what came back against the measurement requirements, while still carrying the hardest task myself. I later compiled the final design report from the sub-teams’ contributions. The team took the project from concept to a working, measuring prototype in about three months, and the client’s reference letter records that he would be glad to work with me again.

Yours sincerely,

Henry Khuc
